# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A farmer or a farm adviser in India, on a phone or a laptop, often outdoors in bright light, deciding which crop follows which over four seasons when irrigation water is limited. Reads plain English. Trusts what shows its source.

## Product purpose

RotationLab compares four-year rotations of rice, jowar, bajra and pigeon pea against real weather records and shows the water level at which land should shift from rice to millets. It supports, and does not replace, farmer and local expert judgment. All outputs are modeled estimates for planning.

## Brand and tone

Plain, warm, exact. Short sentences. Numbers are large and always say where they come from. No hype, no jargon without a gloss, no em dashes.

## Anti-references

Stock Streamlit look, default Plotly styling, purple gradients, green-on-white agriculture cliche, cards inside cards, emoji icons, centered hero, vague filler.

## Design principles

1. Every number carries a provenance badge (Observed, Modeled, Local input, Assumption, Placeholder). The badge shape differs, so color is never the only cue.
2. The answer first, the evidence under it. The tipping chart is the main screen.
3. Regional data is called a regional estimate, never "your field".
4. Uncertainty is shown (percentile bands, bad-year profit), not hidden.
5. Nothing is invented. Missing data is labelled Placeholder or left out.
