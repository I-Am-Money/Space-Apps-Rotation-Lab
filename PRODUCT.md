# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A farmer or a farm adviser in the United States, on a phone or a laptop, often outdoors in bright light, deciding which crop follows which over four years. Reads plain English. Thinks in acres, bushels and inches; hectares are one switch away. Trusts what shows its source. Not a data analyst.

## Product purpose

RotationLab takes a farm's location and current crops, tests four-year crop plans against decades of NASA weather records, and shows three plans next to what the farmer grows today: best for soil and climate, highest profit, best for the soil. It then shows what could change the answer and what is worth checking first. It supports, and does not replace, farmer and local expert judgment. All outputs are modeled estimates for planning.

## Brand and tone

Plain, warm, exact. Short sentences. Numbers are large and always say where they come from. No hype, no jargon without a gloss, no em dashes.

## Anti-references

Stock Streamlit look, default Plotly styling, purple gradients, green-on-white agriculture cliche, cards inside cards, emoji icons, centered hero, vague filler.

## Design principles

1. Every number carries a provenance badge (Observed, Estimated, Your input, Assumption, Example data). The badge shape differs, so color is never the only cue.
2. The answer first, the evidence under it. The three plans come before the controls and the charts.
3. Regional data is called a regional estimate, never "your field".
4. Uncertainty is shown (percentile bands, bad-year profit), not hidden.
5. Nothing is invented. Missing data is labelled Placeholder or left out.
6. Farmer words and farmer units. Corn, not maize. Acres and inches where the farmer uses them. One plain sentence before any table.
